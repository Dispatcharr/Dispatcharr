# Code of Conduct

Dispatcharr welcomes contributions that help people manage content they are authorized to access and distribute. We expect contributors to respect the law, the rights of others, the project's open-source license, and the time of the community that maintains it.

## Contribution standards

1. **Use Dispatcharr lawfully.** Do not contribute code, documentation, or support that facilitates copyright infringement, unauthorized access, credential theft, or the circumvention of access controls.
2. **Protect provider confidentiality and sensitive data.** Do not name or promote providers in public issues, discussions, pull requests, or support channels. Before sharing logs, screenshots, configuration, playlists, or traces, make a best effort to redact provider names, stream URLs, credentials, API keys, tokens, account details, and other identifying or sensitive information.
3. **Respect third-party rights.** Do not submit material that you do not have the right to share. Follow the licenses, terms of service, and acceptable-use policies that apply to third-party libraries, services, content, and APIs.
4. **Keep contributions open source.** Contributions must be compatible with Dispatcharr's [GNU AGPL v3.0 license](LICENSE). Do not add code, assets, or dependencies with terms that conflict with that license or the rights granted in the contributor agreement.
5. **Protect users and their data.** Do not introduce undisclosed telemetry, analytics, licensing checks, phone-home behavior, or external network activity. Any necessary external communication must be clearly documented and handled with appropriate user consent and care for sensitive data.
6. **Use automation responsibly.** Automation, including AI-assisted work, is welcome when it is deliberate, reviewed, tested, and compliant with these standards. You are responsible for confirming that AI-assisted material can be contributed under the project's license, does not improperly reproduce third-party work, and behaves as you describe. AI assistance does not reduce your responsibility to understand and support your contribution.
7. **Participate constructively.** Be respectful in issues, discussions, pull requests, and other community spaces. Keep feedback focused on the work, assume good intent, and do not harass, threaten, discriminate against, or demean others.
8. **Respect people's time and attention.** Search for existing issues and discussions before opening a new one, follow the provided templates, and provide enough context for others to act without repeated clarification. Keep contributions focused, avoid unnecessary notifications or repeated requests for review, and respond thoughtfully to feedback.
9. **Follow the contribution process.** Read and follow [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), including the requirements for focused, tested changes and maintainer approval of large proposals.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report suspected violations privately to a project maintainer through the [Dispatcharr Discord](https://discord.gg/Sp45V5BcxU). Include relevant links or evidence when possible. Do not report security vulnerabilities publicly. Follow the private reporting process in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.

Maintainers have the final say on whether a submission is appropriate for Dispatcharr and how these standards apply. They will review reports with discretion and may remove or reject submissions, close issues or pull requests, restrict participation, or ban contributors when needed to protect the project and its community.

This Code of Conduct guides maintainer decisions and does not limit their authority over repository submissions. Not every situation can be anticipated here, and maintainers may act when conduct or contributions conflict with the project's interests, responsibilities, or standards.
